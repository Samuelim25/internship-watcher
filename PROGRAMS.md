# Chip-design programs, shuttles & scholarships calendar

Emailed every Sunday 10am ET, appended to the digest. Retuned 2026-08-19 for
**FPGA / ASIC digital design, graduating May 2028, Summer 2027 cycle**.
The `digest: true` pagewatchers in `config.json` alert when these pages change —
this file is the hand-maintained calendar that goes out alongside them.

> **Edit this file by hand.** Everything else in the repo is auto-generated.
> Dates below are the historical pattern for each program, not a promise —
> the pagewatchers are what actually catch a window opening.

## ⏰ NEXT 90 DAYS — Aug–Oct 2026

This is the window that matters most: semiconductor firms open Summer 2027
intern reqs from **September through January**, and the biggest names open
earliest and close fastest.

- **NOW — set native job alerts** at AMD, Qualcomm, Apple, TI and Lockheed.
  They use Radancy/Phenom rather than a public feed, so the watcher can only
  page-watch them and the signal is weak. This is the highest-value manual step
  in the whole setup.
- **Sept — NVIDIA, Qualcomm, AMD, Intel, Micron, Marvell intern reqs open.**
  Historically the densest month for ASIC/RTL design intern postings. The
  hourly sweep covers NVIDIA, Micron, Marvell, Intel, Analog Devices, Altera,
  GlobalFoundries and Cadence directly.
- **~Oct: NVIDIA Ignite** — 2-week application window, easy to miss entirely.
  Undergraduate-focused, explicitly includes hardware tracks.
- **Oct 1 – Dec: DoD SMART Scholarship** — full tuition + stipend + guaranteed
  DoD job. The single best fit here for a US chip-design undergrad who is open
  to defense work: SMART placements at Navy (NSWC/NRL) and Air Force Research
  Lab include FPGA and DSP design. Note it commits post-graduation years to DoD.
- **~Sept–Oct: NSA Codebreaker Challenge** launches, runs through the fall.
  School-ranked, clean resume line, and directly relevant to the natsec side.
- **Nov–Jan: defense primes post summer reqs** — Northrop, RTX, Leidos, Draper,
  GDMS. These run later than commercial silicon and are less contested. GDMS
  posts an "FPGA Intern Engineer" req most cycles.
- **Rolling: TinyTapeout shuttle runs** — a genuinely cheap way to have real
  taped-out silicon on a resume before an internship. Runs close on a fixed
  schedule; the pagewatcher tracks the submission window.
- **Dec–Feb: national lab REU deadlines** — DOE SULI, NREIP (Navy), MIT Lincoln
  Lab summer program, Sandia. NREIP and Lincoln Lab both do heavy FPGA/DSP work.

## 🔬 Tapeout & hands-on silicon

Portfolio work matters more for chip-design internships than for SWE, because
undergrad coursework rarely produces anything taped out.

- **TinyTapeout** — <https://tinytapeout.com/> — submit a small digital design
  on a shared shuttle. Cheapest real path to fabricated silicon. Multiple runs
  per year with hard submission deadlines.
- **Efabless chipIgnite** — <https://efabless.com/chipignite> — larger shuttle
  slots, open-source PDK, more substantial than TinyTapeout.
- **Google/SkyWater open MPW** — <https://developers.google.com/silicon> —
  free open-source shuttle program.
- **OpenLane / OpenROAD** — the open RTL-to-GDS flow behind the above. Worth
  knowing even though physical design isn't the target: it makes the design
  constraints legible.
- **FPGA boards** — AMD/Xilinx and Intel/Altera both run university programs
  that donate or discount hardware. Both are page-watched.

## 🏆 Competitions & conference student programs

- **DAC Student Research Forum + Young Fellows** — <https://www.dac.com/> —
  the flagship EDA/design conference. Young Fellows is undergrad-accessible and
  funds travel. Applications typically open in the spring.
- **ISSCC student programs** — <https://www.isscc.org/student-programs> —
  circuits-focused; student travel grants.
- **FPGA (ACM/SIGDA) symposium** — <https://www.isfpga.org/> — the FPGA research
  venue. February, papers due in the fall.
- **FCCM** — <https://www.fccm.org/> — field-programmable custom computing.
  Closest venue to this exact interest, and it runs an undergrad-friendly track.
- **Hot Chips** — <https://hotchips.org/> — August, industry-heavy, cheap for
  students, excellent for recruiting conversations.
- **MICRO / ISCA** — computer architecture; student travel grants.
- **AMD/Xilinx Open Hardware design contest** — <https://www.openhw.eu/> —
  FPGA design competition with a student category.
- **MITRE eCTF** — embedded capture-the-flag, runs Jan–Apr, hardware security.
- **DOE CyberForce** — November, team competition.

## 💰 Scholarships

- **DoD SMART** — see the 90-day list. Biggest single award here.
- **IEEE SSCS** (Solid-State Circuits Society) — <https://sscs.ieee.org/> —
  scholarships and student travel; the most on-target society for chip design.
  Membership has a waiting-period clock, so join early.
- **SEMI Foundation** — <https://www.semi.org/> — semiconductor industry
  workforce scholarships, explicitly aimed at this career path.
- **IEEE CS Merwin** — needs a 3-month IEEE + Computer Society membership clock,
  so join well before the ~Sept 30 deadline.
- **HKN awards (Madni)** — IEEE-HKN honor society; requires induction first.
- **Goldwater** — the research-track scholarship; needs a faculty research
  narrative, which is the long pole. Internal campus deadlines run ~Dec, national
  in the spring. Worth starting a year early.
- **AFCEA STEM Majors** — national, defense-adjacent, low-friction application.
- **DoD Cyber Service Academy** — full ride + service commitment.
- **CHIPS for America workforce programs** —
  <https://www.nist.gov/chips/workforce-development> — new funding streams tied
  to domestic fab buildout; worth watching as programs come online.

## 🏛️ Labs & research programs

The densest source of undergraduate FPGA and DSP design work, and far less
contested than commercial silicon internships.

- **NREIP (Navy)** — <https://navalsteminterns.us/nreip/> — Navy labs, heavy
  radar/SDR/FPGA content. Applications typically open in the fall for summer.
- **MIT Lincoln Laboratory summer program** — radar and signal processing are
  literally the lab's founding specialty. Strong match for the DSP priority.
- **DOE SULI** — <https://science.osti.gov/wdts/suli> — all DOE national labs,
  two cycles per year.
- **Sandia student internships** — microelectronics and radar programs.
- **JHU Applied Physics Lab** — page-watched; large intern program.
- **MIT Haystack REU** — radio science; FPGA-based instrumentation and DSP.
- **NRAO REU** — radio astronomy; genuinely FPGA/DSP-heavy backend work.
- **NSF ETAP** — <https://etap.nsf.gov/> — the searchable index of every NSF REU.
  Filter for ECE; several sites run digital-design projects.
- **SRC (Semiconductor Research Corporation)** — <https://www.src.org/> —
  industry-funded university research; the student center lists member-company
  programs and internships.

## Notes

- **US-only.** The watcher drops non-US roles, including Canada — Tenstorrent
  and Marvell post heavily in Toronto and Ottawa, and those would otherwise
  dominate the list.
- **Clearance.** Many of the defense and lab programs above require US
  citizenship, and some require a clearance the employer sponsors. Roles that
  ask for either are flagged 🇺🇸 in `TOP_PICKS.md`. If you hold or obtain a
  clearance, say so — it changes which of these are worth prioritising.
- **Verification and physical design are filtered out** of the job sweep by
  design. That does not apply to this calendar: a lab program that happens to
  involve verification is still a good program.
